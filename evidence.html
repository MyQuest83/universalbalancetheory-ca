Foundations and selected V3 results

Exact and scoped mathematics

Constant chain and fivefold graphs: The manuscript gives exact identities for θs, σs and πs and a scoped characterization of the C5 trace-normalized combinatorial Laplacian gap for finite connected unweighted simple graphs, plus a distinct conditional optimization result for a restricted weighted graph family. These are mathematical claims with defined assumptions, not an observed universal physical-selection mechanism. See §§4.1, 18.1 and 18.3; the inverse proof is sketched in the paper and its full derivation is retained in a separate research archive.

Kerr transformation: The stationary uncharged Kerr horizon expression, with E=M and the stated natural units, reduces algebraically to ucKerr=2π/√(1−a*2) after mass normalization. This is an exact model-based reduction, not a newly measured Kerr law. See §18.2.

Conditional models and computations

Five-port response model: The response-sector identity in §18.5 is testable under its specified structural conditions. The manuscript reports synthetic passes and rejections. Conventional circuit theory predicts the same identity under those assumptions; a passing model or simulation does not uniquely identify UBT as a physical explanation.

Adverse results retained

ASTRA–DESI: Section 20.5 reports a successful descriptive replay but an AUC gain of approximately 0.000542 against a frozen practical-effect threshold of 0.005, so the practical-effect criterion failed.

Quasicrystal topology: Section 20.12 reports corrected primary C5 counts of 3 instead of 3,461 and a matched-null p-value of 0.1975. The earlier fivefold-enrichment interpretation was retired; a separate historical axis-result discrepancy remains unresolved.

Gravitational waves: Appendix J records the canonical GWTC-5.0 pathway as HOLD_FOR_THEORY / SEALED / NOT RUN. The previously tested uncertainty-calibration limitations remain visible. A sealed plan is not a completed validation.

These are results and statuses reported by the Version 3 manuscript, not an independent replication performed by this website. The report contains additional cases; consult the cited sections rather than treating this guide as a complete experiment registry.

Evidence type is not outcome

Use these labels alongside the recorded outcome (pass, fail, null, ambiguous, blocked, superseded, etc.), tested conditions, source provenance, and claim ceiling. No single label confers universal validation.

  • Conceptual consistency: internal coherence, not empirical measurement.
  • Mathematical result: exact identities and scoped theorems; distinguish proof sketches from complete proofs.
  • Conditional model: conclusions that hold only when the stated assumptions hold.
  • Simulation: computational results under specified model, parameters and code.
  • Observational analysis: analysis of observed or archived data with uncertainty and comparators.
  • Controlled experiment: measured test under documented controls and failure criteria.
  • Independent replication: independent examination of a named result, with methodology and limits.

Sources, versions and access

Current publication: UBT for the World v3, released 19 October 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23473479. Version 2 is retained for historical reference, not as the current edition.

Supporting research: Version 3 §22.4 explains that detailed code, datasets, methods and some derivations may be requested by emailing Michael Eric West. Include the manuscript section and desired material. Availability, permissions and redistribution rights vary; the link is a request route, not a claim that every supporting file is public.

Public participant records: The Trigon registry manifest currently contains no released participant records. The registry is distinct from research documented in the manuscript; draft submissions and private or unreleased archives are not automatically public validation.

Submission: The Trigon Challenge is participant-led. This site is static, with email submission and public-release review, not an account-based submission or automated replication service.

AI evaluations: Record exact prompts, visible model identifiers and settings, raw responses, supplied context, date/time, independent reviewer conditions and subsequent corrections. Use qualitative reasoning by default; numerical confidence must identify its rubric and calibration. AI agreement alone is not independent experimental replication.
