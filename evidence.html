Exact mathematics, with its scope.

Exact algebra

Constants and fixed rescaling

The defined constants satisfy exact algebraic identities. Under identical preprocessing, UCs = σs UCπ. Positive fixed rescaling preserves order, ties and rank-based AUC. A physical mechanism selecting σs remains an open question.

§4.1–4.2, pp. 19–20; §18.1, p. 68
Exact model reduction

Kerr thermodynamics

With E = M, M > 0 and |a*| < 1 in the stated natural units, the stationary, uncharged Kerr model gives ucKerr = 2π / √(1 − a*²). The Schwarzschild limit is 2π. These are exact algebraic consequences within the declared model.

§18.2, p. 68
Scoped theorems

The two C5 results

The inverse theorem characterizes C5 by its trace-normalized Laplacian gap within finite connected unweighted simple graphs with at least two vertices. A separate forward theorem selects uniformly weighted C5 by maximizing that gap among connected triangle-free, non-bipartite degree-two graphs with positive weights of fixed total weight.

The paper includes a proof sketch. The assumptions matter: arbitrary real-weighted graphs admit counterexamples, and physical selection is a separate question.

Computational and observational outcomes.

Conditional model · simulation

Five-site and five-port systems

The five-site relaxation ratio equals σs under the extra matching condition 10κ = γ. The later five-port response identity follows from D(s) = a(s)I + b(s)L5 and does not need that matching condition.

V3 reports 182 synthetic cases with 66 passes and 116 rejections under the recorded gates. Conventional circuit theory predicts the same response identity under the same assumptions. Live circuit measurements remain unrun in this release.

Quasicrystal topology

Correcting an indexing defect changes the primary C5 count from 3,461 to 3, with matched-null p = 0.1975. The primary and directional-robustness criteria fail; the earlier enrichment interpretation is retired.

The corrected empirical result and the scoped mathematical C5 theorems address different claims. The historical axis discrepancy remains unresolved in the manuscript.

Name the evidence type and the outcome.

A result can carry more than one evidence label. Record its outcome separately: supported within scope, failed, null, ambiguous, proposed, unrun or superseded. Light, Medium and Heavy describe test depth. None of these labels is a universal confidence score.

  1. Conceptual consistencyChecks whether stated ideas and definitions fit together; identify any interpretation added by the reviewer.
  2. Mathematical resultDistinguish a definition, exact identity, proof sketch, and complete proof. Keep the assumptions and domain with the result.
  3. Conditional modelStates what follows within a declared model, including imposed parameter relationships and boundary conditions.
  4. Simulation or numerical reproductionReports computational results for specified code, inputs and settings; identify what was reproduced and what was not run.
  5. Observational analysisReports findings from measured data, with provenance, uncertainty, comparators and the limits of the study design.
  6. Controlled experimentReports a physical intervention and measured response under recorded controls and conditions.
  7. Independent replicationIdentifies who independently repeated which result, using what method or data, and with what outcome. Agreement between AI reviews alone does not establish replication.

Credit successful results within their demonstrated scope. Preserve failed checks, uncertainty and corrections with the same care. Independent AI reviews may help identify problems; their agreement does not substitute for independent experimental replication.
