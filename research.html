01V3 §20.1

Exact mathematics and graph theory

Exact constants and a complete small-graph enumeration agree within their declared scope. Weighted counterexamples keep the assumptions visible.

Reported outcomeExact checks supported within scope

Mathematical resultComputational enumeration
Question, method & result+

The question

Which graph patterns retain the specified spectral relationship when the graph class and normalization are fixed?

How it was tested

The archived run checked the exact constant certificate and independently enumerated all labelled simple graphs on two through six vertices. It also tested a weighted three-vertex path as a counterexample.

What the paper reports

  • The certificate verified 80 decimal places of the published constant and checked 100 continued-fraction convergents.
  • Of 33,866 labelled simple graphs, 27,475 were connected. Exactly 12 matched the specified normalized first positive eigenvalue; these are the 12 labellings of the five-cycle C5.
  • 72 graphs contained the constant somewhere in the trace-normalized spectrum, while only 12 had it as the gap. The stronger all-size inverse theorem and its assumptions are discussed separately in Chapter 18.

Scope & limitations

  • A finite enumeration establishes a finite search result; the all-size claim relies on its mathematical proof.
  • Arbitrary real-weighted graphs admit a different graph with the same scalar, so the unweighted assumption matters.
  • These mathematical results do not themselves identify a physical selection mechanism.

What this teaches

A precise mathematical result is strongest when its assumptions and counterexamples travel with it.

The paper's next step

State the graph class, allowed weights, normalization and equality conditions. Reproduce the C5 calculation and weighted counterexample, then test a declared stability bound on held-out perturbations.

02V3 §20.2

Circuits and control systems

Twenty simulated five-node RC trajectories closely match the modal solution. The preferred relaxation ratio depends on a stated component-matching condition.

Reported outcomeNumerical agreement; bench test unrun

Conditional modelSimulation
Question, method & result+

The question

How closely does a five-node circuit simulation match its analytic modes, and which response relationships survive a change in component settings?

How it was tested

An independent fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration was compared with the modal solution for 20 five-node RC trajectories, using the recorded capacitance and conductances.

What the paper reports

  • The maximum voltage disagreement was about 5.90 × 10⁻¹³ V.
  • The ratio giving the published scale factor appears with the imposed conductance match G₀ = 10G₁ and disappears when that match is changed.
  • The paper separately proposes a calibrated five-port response experiment, with frequency holdouts and distance-two connections as a negative control.

Scope & limitations

  • This is a reproducible realization within ordinary circuit theory.
  • It is not a live bench measurement or an advantage over ordinary component-level predictions.
  • The relaxation matching condition and the five-port structural identity are different results. The latter does not require that same match.

What this teaches

A circuit can test a sharply defined model while revealing exactly which component choices produce its behavior.

The paper's next step

Measure the five-port response with frozen port order, frequency bands and calibration. Check reciprocity, leakage, isotropy, conditioning and uncertainty before evaluating the structural prediction.

03V3 §20.3

Thermodynamics and energy systems

A ground-referenced quantity is invariant to an additive energy shift in exact algebra. The direct finite-precision implementation still fails its numerical tolerance.

Reported outcomeExact invariance; numerical and predictive limits

Mathematical resultNumerical benchmark
Question, method & result+

The question

Can an energy-based diagnostic retain the same physical meaning when the arbitrary energy zero changes?

How it was tested

The complete three-level benchmark was rerun on 4,001 temperatures with frozen energy shifts and compared with high-precision arithmetic.

What the paper reports

  • The ground-referenced construction cancels an additive energy shift exactly in algebra.
  • Its direct binary64 implementation has maximum residual about 6.77285 × 10⁻⁷, exceeding the stated 10⁻¹¹ tolerance.
  • The reference-temperature residual has a larger maximum error, about 0.0268536. The predictive comparison remains adverse.

Scope & limitations

  • Exact invariance and numerical stability are separate properties.
  • Low temperatures and extreme offsets expose cancellation error.
  • A numerical repair would still need its own predictive comparison.

What this teaches

Correct algebra earns credit even when a particular implementation or predictive use fails.

The paper's next step

Derive a stable implementation, freeze offsets and temperature range, and test unseen cases against a high-precision oracle before making a predictive interpretation.

04V3 §20.4

Astronomy and gravitational waves

A bounded replay reproduces six M87 fits and a matching synthetic ring. Curvature identifiability and gravitational-wave uncertainty calibration remain unresolved.

Reported outcomeBounded replay supported; calibration incomplete

Observational analysisSynthetic instrument checks
Question, method & result+

The question

What can a limited astronomical signal actually distinguish, and how reliable are the resulting fitted estimates?

How it was tested

One preserved official M87 visibility product was reparsed with a declared short-baseline cut. Six same-file fits and synthetic positive and wrong-model controls were reproduced. The pathway also retains the separate historical GW250114 injection study.

What the paper reports

  • The M87 file had 6,458 rows: 5,865 retained, 593 excluded, and no invalid rows. Six fits reproduce the historical results for that file.
  • A matching model recovers a noiseless 40 μas ring, while a Gaussian control leaves about 0.119 Jy RMS residual.
  • The full historical M87 harness recovered 191 of 192 correct-model noiseless cases and remains PARTIAL.
  • The GW250114 study included 48 injections in six design groups sharing eight noise contexts. Nominal 95% frequency support covered truth in only 4 of 8 SNR-20 trials at the +5 ms start.

Scope & limitations

  • The one-product replay does not reproduce the full historical 64-file roster.
  • The amplitude-model checks do not identify spacetime curvature change.
  • The successful small replay does not clear the full-harness failures; the historical GW coverage failure was not reevaluated in this replay.

What this teaches

A trustworthy instrument must show both what it can recover and what its measurements cannot distinguish.

The paper's next step

For M87, establish an identifiable measurement-to-curvature relation or retain the morphology claim. For gravitational waves, freeze a larger injection and noise roster and repair uncertainty calibration.

05V3 §20.5

Cosmology and cosmic structure

The V-S-F-K chain retains first place in the descriptive replay. Its positive AUC increment misses the frozen practical-effect threshold, and external prediction remains adverse.

Reported outcomeDescriptive support; practical criteria failed

Observational analysisSaved-table recalculation
Question, method & result+

The question

Does a descriptive pattern in the cosmic web provide useful predictive information beyond catalogue construction and conventional comparators?

How it was tested

The hash-matched ASTRA-DESI v0.12 probability archive was replayed for 12 chains across 15 strata. An explicit path-sum implementation checked the ordering. Saved practical-effect and transfer tables were recalculated.

What the paper reports

  • Of 603,914 rows, 602,273 were valid and 1,641 zero-sum rows were excluded; no duplicate region/tracer/TARGETID keys were found.
  • V-S-F-K again ranks first with ratio 1.95778284223, and the independent path-sum implementation agrees exactly.
  • The AUC gain is +0.0005423793 against a required +0.005. Signs are positive in all eight transfers, but the useful-effect gate fails.
  • Weighted external log-SFR changes are ΔMAE ≈ −9.57623 × 10⁻⁶ and ΔR² ≈ −2.28433 × 10⁻⁵, with beneficial signs in one of four transfers.

Scope & limitations

  • The predictive models were not retrained during this replay.
  • In the recovered code, ΔMAE means baseline error minus augmented-model error; a negative value is worse.
  • The revealed archive is development data for a future test, not a fresh blind dataset.

What this teaches

Keep a reproducible descriptive success beside its failed stronger test, so readers can see exactly what survived.

The paper's next step

Pin features, construction variables and training settings for a complete predictive replay. A new release should reserve whole regions or releases and declare a useful effect size before inspecting outcomes.

06V3 §20.6

Structural biology and biophysics

One graph-score comparison passes, but quality and specificity limits keep the seven-structure biological result at NULL / INSUFFICIENT.

Reported outcomeComponent pass; overall insufficient

Observational structure analysisComputational replay
Question, method & result+

The question

Can a reproducible structural graph result be linked to a separately measured biological function?

How it was tested

All seven PRX structure files were reacquired from RCSB and matched their historical hashes. The original extraction, quality-control and scoring workflows were rerun.

What the paper reports

  • Four entries could be scored, two failed the original quality gates, and one had too few dimers.
  • The P2 graph-score margin of 0.054043709289 exceeds its frozen threshold of 0.027431633896.
  • P1 topology/quality control fails and P3 specificity cannot be evaluated, so the overall outcome remains NULL / INSUFFICIENT.
  • The four valid gap values reproduce exactly; tiny eigenvalue differences are within 5.56 × 10⁻¹⁷.

Scope & limitations

  • The passing component is not the overall result.
  • Uniform scaling of graph weights preserves the normalized spectrum and does not create an independent biological signal.
  • Homologous structures and repeated graph nodes must not be counted as automatically independent evidence.

What this teaches

A mixed result deserves a complete denominator: the passed comparison, failed controls, exclusions and unavailable check all matter.

The paper's next step

Choose an observable beyond graph construction, freeze extraction and quality rules, compare with an ordinary biological model, and reserve entire proteins or batches.

07V3 §20.7

Mechanics and acoustics

The generator matches an independent recurrence but not the published sequence windows. A simple input-derived rule reconstructs every published label for the second target.

Reported outcomeSource mismatch and target leakage identified

Source auditComputational benchmark
Question, method & result+

The question

Are the archived motion data, generating equation and prediction target consistent with the physical question?

How it was tested

The variable-length pendulum archive was downloaded and its publisher checksum verified. The saved generator was compared with an independent recurrence and the archived windows; target labels were tested directly against supplied inputs.

What the paper reports

  • The configured generator does not reproduce the archived windows: maximum feature discrepancies are 13.3393 and 11.6109 in the two segments. The frozen result is SOURCE MISMATCH.
  • The final length entry is zero. The generator also omits a term required by the standard prescribed-length equation unless an additional tangential force is supplied.
  • The sign of the last supplied angle-times-angular-velocity reconstructs all 382 published second-target labels, versus saved RNN accuracies of about 90.576% and 75.916%.
  • A 31-position shift control drops to about 67.539% in each segment, checking alignment without creating an independent physical target.

Scope & limitations

  • These are source and model limitations, not measured physical failures of UBT.
  • A target already encoded in the inputs cannot demonstrate new predictive information.
  • Acoustic transfer remains a separate future test.

What this teaches

Before optimizing a model, make sure its data and target measure the question you meant to ask.

The paper's next step

Resolve generator provenance, derive the intended dynamics and use positive lengths throughout. Predict a genuinely future quantity and hold out complete trajectories and length bands.

08V3 §20.8

Power-grid engineering

All repaired AC power-flow cases converge, but the simple voltage-screening surrogate misses every held-out low-voltage violation.

Reported outcomeSolver repaired; safety screening failed

SimulationHeld-out engineering benchmark
Question, method & result+

The question

Can a simple screening model recognize voltage violations across increased loads and a different network?

How it was tested

Two SimBench rural low-voltage networks were solved at 41 load factors each. A quadratic minimum-voltage surrogate trained on 21 low-load cases was evaluated on 61 higher-load or different-network cases.

What the paper reports

  • All 82 repaired AC power-flow cases converge after the disclosed solver-initialization change, which preserves the transformer phase shift.
  • The surrogate has MAE 0.0275626 per unit, misses all 13 lower-voltage violations, and raises no false alarms.
  • No test case exceeds the 1.05 upper bound, so upper-bound detection sensitivity cannot be assessed.
  • Refitting after a constant feature rescaling changes predictions by less than 10⁻¹⁵ per unit.

Scope & limitations

  • The original failed batch did not retain individual case logs; per-case attempts cover the repaired run.
  • Zero false alarms cannot compensate for missing every observed hazard.
  • A simulation benchmark is not a qualified operating-grid system.

What this teaches

Engineering usefulness depends on the decisions a score gets right, especially the hazards it misses.

The paper's next step

Use independently available network inputs, compare with conventional AC power flow, calibrate screening on training data and reserve entire topology families and time profiles.

09V3 §20.9

Ecology and climate

The original NEON flux protocol is unrun. A separate NOAA forecast comparison finds that recent seasonal drift beats the longer-history linear model.

Reported outcomeSeasonal baseline wins; NEON unrun

Observational forecasting benchmarkUnrun protocol
Question, method & result+

The question

Which baseline best predicts the next month in the selected environmental time series, and what does that say about the separate ecosystem-flux proposal?

How it was tested

A separately frozen benchmark used 552 monthly NOAA global marine-surface CO₂ observations from 1980–2025, with 432 initial training months and 120 successive held-out one-month forecasts.

What the paper reports

  • Seasonal-drift MAE is 0.614167 ppm, compared with 2.59725 ppm for seasonal naive and 5.01401 ppm for the expanding-window trend with month indicators.
  • Refitting a constant-scaled time feature changes predictions by only 1.756 × 10⁻⁹ ppm.
  • The month-indicator rotation control does not worsen aggregate MAE in the misspecified model and gives no positive seasonal-efficacy result.
  • The separate NEON ecosystem-flux protocol remains unrun after its documented endpoint returned HTTP 403.

Scope & limitations

  • Later fits can use earlier evaluation months; this was not a ten-year forecast issued in 2015.
  • The analysis used a revised data product and is retrospective, rather than a real-time historical-vintage test.
  • Atmospheric concentration and ecosystem exchange are different targets.

What this teaches

A simple, well-matched baseline can be more useful than a complicated model, and one environmental dataset cannot substitute for another.

The paper's next step

Complete the separate NEON protocol when files and quality flags are available. Define observed targets apart from gap-filled estimates and reserve whole years and sites.

10V3 §20.10

Institutions and economics

An ordinary monthly regression beats a seasonal-naive forecast for Toronto 311 totals. Constant rescaling leaves the refitted predictions unchanged.

Reported outcomeConventional forecast improvement reproduced

Administrative-data analysisRolling forecast benchmark
Question, method & result+

The question

How well can ordinary forecasts anticipate monthly public-service request counts, and what can those counts legitimately tell us?

How it was tested

Five official Toronto 311 annual files were parsed into 60 monthly totals. Starting with 36 training months, trend-plus-month regression and seasonal naive were compared over 24 successive monthly targets.

What the paper reports

  • The files contain 2,118,429 rows from 2021–2025; all dates parse, and the 2025 source requires CP1252 decoding.
  • Ordinary regression gives MAE 5,161.54 requests versus 5,692.04 for seasonal naive, a descriptive improvement of about 9.32%.
  • Refitting with a constant-scaled trend changes predictions by about 1.106 × 10⁻⁹ requests. The reported improvement belongs to the ordinary forecasting comparison.

Scope & limitations

  • Each one-month forecast is fitted only to earlier months, not frozen as a two-year forecast at the start.
  • Citywide totals cover only part of demand and do not resolve categories, duplicates, geography changes, closure dates or wellbeing.
  • Repeated rows are not independent experiments.

What this teaches

Useful public-service forecasting is a concrete result; claims about welfare or resource outcomes require those outcomes to be measured.

The paper's next step

Choose a stable service category and geography, establish coverage and deduplication rules, compare with an appropriate count model and freeze a final period and useful error reduction.

11V3 §20.11

Computing and information

Nine published keyword-spotting submissions meet the accuracy requirement. The audit preserves timing precision and missing energy data; fixed rescaling changes no ranking.

Reported outcomeSource comparison reproduced within limits

Published benchmark-log audit
Question, method & result+

The question

Can published device results be compared fairly without changing metric definitions or filling in missing measurements?

How it was tested

The audit reanalyzed all nine paired Closed Division keyword-spotting submissions in the pinned MLPerf Tiny v1.3 repository, reading the paired accuracy and timing records.

What the paper reports

  • All nine submissions satisfy the 90% accuracy threshold.
  • Inverse median throughput ranges from about 0.06495 to 48.30157 milliseconds per inference, with median about 4.3672.
  • Five submissions report energy and four remain missing. Two timing records are text-only and keep their published precision.
  • Multiplication by the positive published scale factor preserves every ranking.

Scope & limitations

  • These are published device logs, not hardware executions performed for the book or an independent verification of vendor timing.
  • Reciprocal throughput is an average time-per-inference measure under the submitted procedure; it is not tail latency.
  • Configurations from one vendor are not independent scientific replications.

What this teaches

Fair comparison begins with honest metric names, complete quality gates and visible missing data.

The paper's next step

Pin workload, quality, hardware, software and measurement boundary. If physical performance is claimed, test the same devices under a frozen procedure and preserve uncertainty and missing values.

12V3 §20.12

Materials and the quantum and chemistry boundary

Correcting a graph-indexing defect removes the earlier large fivefold enrichment. Separate axis analyses remain adverse and numerically inconsistent.

Reported outcomeCorrection verified; fivefold criteria failed

Observational material analysisSource and indexing auditCorrected computational replay
Question, method & result+

The question

Does the proposed fivefold structure remain supported when reflection identities, graph indices and matched-null comparisons are checked?

How it was tested

The frozen 4,057-reflection IQC input and scorer were checked, axis results replayed, and an indexing defect repaired. An independent mapping check and all 1,999 primary matched-null rows were reproduced.

What the paper reports

  • Archived twofold/threefold/fivefold axis counts are 17/13/21; the replay gives 12/10/20. Both axis analyses fail the fivefold gate, and the numerical discrepancy remains unresolved.
  • Correct mapping changes the primary 10% tail C3/C4/C5/C6 counts from 2,762/992/3,461/11,314 to 65/10/3/0.
  • The corrected primary C5 result is z = 0.96659 and p = 0.1975. At 5%, 10% and 20% tails, C5 counts are 0, 3 and 33 with p-values 1.0, 0.1975 and 0.698.
  • Primary and directional-robustness criteria fail. The former enrichment interpretation is retired as an indexing artifact, with historical outputs retained.

Scope & limitations

  • The two adverse axis executions are not numerically reproduced; floating-point sensitivity is a possible cause with no established mechanism.
  • No new specimen or held-out material prediction is introduced, and the topology repair does not complete a revised full pipeline.
  • The exploratory C4 p-value of 0.019 is not a multiplicity-corrected discovery or fivefold support.
  • Chemical applications and new quantum measurements remain unrun; a separate synthetic quantum-clock example is not their validation.

What this teaches

Publishing a correction improves the reliability of the record, even when it removes an attractive earlier result.

The paper's next step

Preserve row identities through sorting and graph operations, test invariance to input order, recover direct publisher provenance for the mirrored input, and reserve a new specimen for a successor prediction.
